# The Text chapter: moving over and acting on words, sentences,
# paragraphs, pages and quotation marks. It links only commands that exist
# in the registry, so the manual and the command docs stay in step (see
# Part 2 of the documentation design).

** Text
   :PROPERTIES:
   :ID:       Text
   :CUSTOM_ID: Text
   :SUMMARY:  Move over and act on words, sentences, paragraphs and pages
   :COMMANDS: forward-word backward-word kill-word backward-kill-word
              mark-word transpose-words count-words count-words-region
              forward-sentence backward-sentence kill-sentence
              backward-kill-sentence forward-paragraph backward-paragraph
              mark-paragraph forward-page backward-page mark-page
              count-lines-page what-page
              electric-quote-mode electric-quote-local-mode
   :END:

Text in Forge is worked on at the size of words, sentences, paragraphs and
pages. Each has commands to move, to kill and to mark, and each takes an
argument to repeat it (see [[*Keys and commands][Keys and commands]]).

*** Words
   :PROPERTIES:
   :SUMMARY:  Move over, kill and transpose words
   :COMMANDS: forward-word backward-word kill-word backward-kill-word
              mark-word transpose-words count-words count-words-region
   :END:

~M-f~ (~forward-word~) moves point forward over a word and ~M-b~
(~backward-word~) moves it back. A negative argument moves the other way.
Forward motion stops just after the last character of the word; backward
motion stops just before the first.

~M-d~ (~kill-word~) kills from point to where ~M-f~ would move to, so a
word in the middle loses only the part after point, and any punctuation
before the next word goes with it. ~M-DEL~ (~backward-kill-word~) kills
back to where ~M-b~ would move to. Both take an argument.

~M-@~ (~mark-word~) sets the mark where ~M-f~ would move to and leaves
point where it is, so the region is the next word. With an argument it
marks that many words.

~M-t~ (~transpose-words~) exchanges the word before or containing point
with the one after it; the punctuation between them stays put, so ~FOO, BAR~
becomes ~BAR, FOO~. If there is no word after point, it transposes the two
words before it.

A word is the run of letters, digits and underscores. ~M-=~
(~count-words-region~) counts lines, words and characters in the region,
and ~M-x count-words~ counts the whole buffer when there is no region.

*** Sentences
   :PROPERTIES:
   :SUMMARY:  Move over sentences with M-a, M-e and M-k
   :COMMANDS: forward-sentence backward-sentence kill-sentence
              backward-kill-sentence
   :END:

~M-a~ (~backward-sentence~) moves to the beginning of the current sentence
and ~M-e~ (~forward-sentence~) to the end of it. They move over successive
sentences when repeated.

A sentence is text that finishes with ~.~, ~?~ or ~!~, optionally followed by
closing quotes or brackets, and then whitespace or the end of the buffer.
Going backward puts point on the sentence's first character; going forward
puts it right after the closing punctuation, ahead of the whitespace.

By default Forge counts a single space after a period as a sentence break.
To require two spaces (or a line break), as typewriter-era text does, set
~forge.opt["sentence_end_double_space"]~. Languages that don't end sentences
with a period, such as Thai, need ~forge.opt["sentence_end_without_period"]~.
For full control, ~forge.opt["sentence_end"]~ takes your own regexp for
"the end of a sentence plus the whitespace that follows".

~M-k~ (~kill-sentence~) deletes from point through the end of the sentence
and saves it on the kill ring. A numeric argument of ~N~ repeats that for ~N~
sentences; a negative one works backward instead. ~C-x DEL~
(~backward-kill-sentence~) deletes back to the beginning of the sentence.

*** Paragraphs
   :PROPERTIES:
   :SUMMARY:  Move over paragraphs and mark one with M-h
   :COMMANDS: forward-paragraph backward-paragraph mark-paragraph
   :END:

~M-{~ (~backward-paragraph~) goes to the top of the paragraph you are in, or
the one before it when you are already at the top. ~M-}~ (~forward-paragraph~)
goes to the bottom of the current paragraph, or the next one. A blank line
in front of the paragraph is where ~M-{~ lands.

A paragraph is a run of lines that are not blank; a blank line separates
paragraphs. ~forge.opt["paragraph_start"]~ and ~forge.opt["paragraph_separate"]~
are regexps that decide which lines start a paragraph and which separate
them. ~M-h~ (~mark-paragraph~) sets point at the start and the
mark at the end of the paragraph around point. With an argument ~N~ it
marks ~N~ paragraphs from here.

*** Pages
   :PROPERTIES:
   :SUMMARY:  Move over pages, and mark or count one
   :COMMANDS: forward-page backward-page mark-page count-lines-page what-page
   :END:

A page is delimited by a formfeed (~C-q C-l~ inserts one), or by whatever
~forge.opt["page_delimiter"]~ matches at the start of a line. ~C-x ]~
(~forward-page~) moves point to the next delimiter and ~C-x [~
(~backward-page~) to the previous one. ~M-x what-page~ shows the page
number and the line within it; ~C-x l~ (~count-lines-page~) shows how many
lines the page has, split before and after point. ~C-x C-p~ (~mark-page~)
puts point at the start of the page and the mark at its end; a numeric
argument chooses a page relative to the current one.

*** Quotation Marks
   :PROPERTIES:
   :SUMMARY:  Type curved quotes with Electric Quote mode
   :COMMANDS: electric-quote-mode electric-quote-local-mode
   :END:

Electric Quote mode turns ~`~ into a left single quote and ~'~ into a right
single quote; ~``~ becomes a left double quote and ~''~ a right one. It is
off by default. ~M-x electric-quote-local-mode~ toggles it in one buffer and
~M-x electric-quote-mode~ in every buffer; a prefix argument turns it on.
~C-q `~ and ~C-q '~ type the character unchanged.

Electric Quote mode is active in text paragraphs and comments by default
and not in strings. ~forge.opt["electric_quote_chars"]~ names the four quotes
it types, ~forge.opt["electric_quote_replace_double"]~ makes ~"~ type a curved
double quote, and ~forge.opt["electric_quote_paragraph"]~,
~forge.opt["electric_quote_comment"]~ and ~forge.opt["electric_quote_string"]~ say
where it applies.

To insert a curved quote without turning the mode on, type ~C-x 8 [~ for a
left single quote, ~C-x 8 ]~ for a right one, ~C-x 8 {~ for a left double
and ~C-x 8 }~ for a right one.
